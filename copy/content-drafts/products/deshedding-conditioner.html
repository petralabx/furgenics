Why a deshedding-specific conditioner matters in the protocol

A deshedding shampoo loosens undercoat during the bath. The conditioner does the second-act work: lubricates the coat enough that the undercoat actually releases under the HV dryer or slicker rather than re-tangling, while strengthening the topcoat for weather resistance between grooms.

Hydrolyzed keratin is the carry-over from the shampoo — the protein fragments continue penetrating damaged sections of the hair shaft during the conditioner's contact time, where shampoo contact alone wasn't enough. Argan oil handles the slip — light enough to coat individual hairs without weighing down the undercoat (which would trap shedding hair rather than release it). Shea butter adds finishing conditioning to the topcoat without going into the undercoat layer.

The balance matters. Heavy oils in a deshedding conditioner are counterproductive — they coat the undercoat and prevent release. The lighter conditioning chemistry here is purpose-built for the deshedding protocol, not a generic conditioner with "deshedding" on the label.

How to dilute and use

This is the conditioner step in the deshedding protocol. Use after Furgenics Deshedding Shampoo on the same bath.

  1. Confirm shampoo is fully rinsed before applying conditioner. Residual shampoo plus conditioner over loose undercoat creates the worst-case mat scenario during dry-out.
  2. Dilute at [[VALUE:dilution-ratio]] in a mixing bottle. Heavy seasonal blowout coats can take ready-to-use undiluted on the densest undercoat areas (shoulders, hindquarters, tail base).
  3. Apply along the back and work down into undercoat areas, massaging through the coat with hands. Pay particular attention to undercoat-heavy zones rather than treating it as a topcoat-only product.
  4. Leave on 3–5 minutes. Keratin needs contact time to bond with the hair shaft; shortening this step skips the strengthening effect.
  5. Rinse fully until water runs clear. Retained conditioner traps loose undercoat during the HV-dry phase.
  6. HV dry against the lay of the coat, working the dryer nozzle close to skin. This is where the bulk of dead undercoat actually releases — expect 1–2 cups of dead undercoat on a heavy seasonal blowout.
  7. Finish with a slicker brush across the topcoat for any straggling hair.
