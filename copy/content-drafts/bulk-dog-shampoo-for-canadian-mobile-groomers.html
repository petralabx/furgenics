What mobile groomers actually need from a bulk shampoo

Mobile grooming has constraints that a brick-and-mortar salon doesn't. The product has to solve for all of them.

Weight and volume. A gallon of shampoo weighs roughly 3.8 kg. In a van stocked for a full day, every product fights for space and adds mass. High-concentration formulas ([[VALUE:dilution-ratio]], 32:1, or 50:1) win this trade by definition — one gallon of [[VALUE:dilution-ratio]] yields [[VALUE:working-gallons-per-bottle]]. You carry less weight, store less volume, and reorder less often.

Water quality variability. Mobile groomers hook up to client-side water supplies that range from hard, iron-rich rural wells to heavily chlorinated municipal city water. A shampoo that foams predictably across that range matters more than in a fixed salon with filtered water. Mild, pH-balanced hypoallergenic formulas are the safest bet.

Dog variety. A mobile route in an average week handles doodles, shepherds, retrievers, terriers, small breeds, and occasional senior dogs with skin issues. A single everyday shampoo that's safe for all of them is far more practical than stocking six specialty products. A hypoallergenic or oatmeal-based mild formula plus a specialty deshedding shampoo for heavy shedders covers most weekly routes.

Domestic supply reliability. Mobile groomers can't afford to be out of stock when the next morning's bookings are already set. Sourcing from a domestic warehouse avoids cross-border shipping delays, customs paperwork, and currency conversion. Brands fulfilled domestically in your market — Furgenics serves Canadian orders from Vaughan, Ontario and US orders via a US fulfillment partner — offer meaningfully faster restock cycles than brands shipping cross-border.

Per-wash cost. The real cost to watch isn't bottle price — it's cost per dog washed. A ready-to-use gallon of shampoo washes roughly 40 dogs at typical usage (3 oz per medium dog). A [[VALUE:dilution-ratio]] gallon, diluted properly, washes around 340 dogs at the same per-dog usage. The per-wash cost difference is roughly 8-to-1 in concentrate's favor before any brand-specific price comparison.

How do I dilute [[VALUE:dilution-ratio]] in a bottle on the van?
Fill a dedicated squeeze or foamer bottle most of the way with lukewarm water, then add shampoo to reach the 16:1 ratio (for a 32 oz bottle: 2 oz shampoo + 30 oz water). Shake gently to mix; avoid vigorous shaking to prevent excess foaming. Use within 2–3 weeks of dilution for best results.
