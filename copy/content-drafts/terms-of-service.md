## Internal notes (don't include in the published terms)

- The decision to use Ontario governing law with **non-exclusive** jurisdiction (rather than exclusive) is deliberate. Non-exclusive lets Furgenics sue customers in any jurisdiction where they have assets while still preferring Ontario as the home venue. Exclusive Ontario jurisdiction would force Furgenics to also litigate in Ontario even when it would be cheaper to sue a defaulting US customer in their home state. Lawyer should confirm this aligns with strategy.
- The 12-month / CAD $100 liability cap is calibrated for a consumer e-commerce business selling individual gallons. For wholesale transactions where a single order might be $5,000+, the 12-month-purchase floor protects Furgenics adequately. For one-off retail customers with $25 orders, the CAD $100 ceiling may actually be higher than 12 months of purchases. The greater-of structure is intentional.
- The class-action waiver without mandatory arbitration is a deliberate middle ground. Mandatory arbitration is the most aggressive merchant-protective posture but is increasingly challenged in Canada. A class-action-only waiver is enforceable in many contexts and avoids the arbitration-clause case law. A lawyer can strengthen this if desired.
- The User Content licence in Section 13 is broad (worldwide, royalty-free, perpetual, irrevocable, sublicensable). This is standard for e-commerce ToS but is at the aggressive end. For Groomer Program participants who post photos with the brand, this licence lets Furgenics reuse those photos without separate consent. Lawyer should review whether this is enforceable as a click-wrap term against professional groomers.
- Section 19 (Termination) lets Furgenics terminate without cause. This is enforceable but unusual to invoke without reason. The bigger value is the explicit list of acceptable reasons in Sections 5 and 6 (suspected fraud, suspected reseller, etc.) which give Furgenics stronger ground when terminating for those specific reasons.
- I did NOT include a separate "Notice for California residents" section (CCPA-related disclosures usually live in the privacy policy, not the ToS). The privacy policy will need that. Same for Quebec Bill 25 / Law 25 disclosures.
- I did NOT include sanctions-list / export-control language. Furgenics ships only to Canada and US currently, both of which are sanctions-light for cosmetic-grade dog shampoos. If the business expands internationally, this section gets added.
